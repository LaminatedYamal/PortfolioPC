1. Introduction 
This internship was designed with the central focus on managing traffic campaigns 
paid (Paid Media), integrating, according to the initial plan, a component 
comprehensive support in web development, email marketing and communications 
organization for the educational group (Universidade Lusófona, IPLUSO, ISMAT, ISLA-IPGT, 
ISLA Santarém and ISDOM). My initial objective was clear: accept all challenges 
proposed and maximize learning about all of the operations of the 
department. However, in the first few weeks, immersion in operational reality 
revealed a systemic bottleneck: the manual management of a portfolio of more than 300 
courses, combined with the multiplicity of support tasks, made execution 
unsustainable conventional. 
 
The need that motivated the intervention was administrative inefficiency in the face of 
load peaks. When mass launching campaigns, the manual process of 
insertion of headlines and descriptions became a critical bottleneck, requiring 
dozens of hours of continuous and exhaustive team effort. This model of 
work, in addition to being unsustainable, opened room for human errors and prevented 
team to focus on strategic analysis. 
 
Recognizing that most of these tasks required repetitive execution and 
exhaustive, I decided to pivot my approach: automate all tasks with AI 
of low cognitive value. My intervention evolved from an operative execution 
for building a Marketing Operations (MarkOps) infrastructure. O 
The objective became the creation of an automation system that supported the 
management of these multiple fronts, ensuring the technical integrity of the ecosystem, 
through proactive link monitoring, data hygiene and security scripts 
validation, freeing up resources for strategic thinking.

Theoretical Framework 
The theoretical foundation of this report is based on the evolution of paradigms of 
digital communication, exploring the concepts that support communication methodologies 
capture, conversion and automation applied during the internship. 
1. The Evolution of Marketing: From Institutional to Marketing 5.0 
The Higher Education ecosystem has historically operated under a logic of 
institutional and passive reputation marketing, strongly anchored in tactics 
Top of Funnel (TOFU) generalists. However, the consumer journey 
Contemporary demands a more sophisticated approach. According to Kotler, Kartajaya and 
Setiawan (2021), the transition to "Marketing 5.0" dictates the application of technologies 
that mimic human behavior to create, communicate and deliver value. 
Today, the enrollment decision of a potential student is not dictated solely by 
static prestige of the institution, but due to the technical capacity of the brand in 
intercept your search intent in an agile and personalized way (Kotler et al., 
2021). This evolution underlies the urgency of transitioning from a reactive operation 
for a data-driven infrastructure. 
2. Performance Marketing and SEO: Visibility and Conversion 
In the field of Digital Marketing, effectiveness is measured by the ability to generate 
tangible results through the strategic management of digital channels. Chaffey and 
Ellis-Chadwick (2019) emphasize that the success of the online presence depends on the 
optimizing the intersection between user intent and brand offering, 
transforming the digital ecosystem into a surgical conversion engine. No 
context of search engines, this approach requires an exhaustive search of 
keywords and continuous monitoring, applied both to 
paid advertising (Search Engine Marketing - SEM) and organic traffic (Search

=== PAGE 14 ===
Engine Optimization - SEO). Ryan (2016) reinforces that the alignment between the 
investment in ads and organic authority guarantees that the institution does not 
acquires only volume of traffic, but qualified relevance, maximizing the 
Return on Investment (ROI). 
3. Data Analytics and Integration: From Noise to Actionable Decision 
In the context of digital analytics, the true usefulness of data does not lie in its 
mere accumulation, but in the ability to extract actionable insights. Kaushik (2010) 
argues that organizations must move away from obsessing over performance metrics 
vanity and focusing on data that drives business decisions. This premise 
theory supports the development of integrated analytical infrastructures 
(crossing tools such as Google Ads and Google Search Console). When processing 
these metrics centrally, technical SEO and campaign management 
cease to be empirical tasks and become strictly 
driven by real performance data, allowing route corrections in time 
useful. 
4. Direct Communication and Loyalty: Permission Marketing 
Lead capture and subsequent email communication should 
move away from intrusive logic. The concept of "Permission Marketing", coined 
by Godin (1999), is based on the privilege of sending advance messages, 
personal and relevant to individuals who have expressed a voluntary desire for the 
receive. This theory underlies the need to design forms of 
optimized capture that reduces friction in the user journey and allows 
primary segmentation. By applying segmentation right at the moment of conversion, 
institution ensures that direct communication via email marketing is highly 
relevant, which, according to Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick (2019), increases 
significantly increase open rates and conversion likelihood over the course of 
digital funnel.

5. Artificial Intelligence in Operational Marketing 
The most recent revolution in operational marketing concerns the adoption of 
Artificial Intelligence (AI). The integration of AI agents and language models 
large scale (LLMs) enable the automation of repetitive, low-value tasks 
cognitive. Davenport, Guha, Grewal and Bressgott (2020) highlight that AI in 
marketing frees professionals to think strategically, assuming 
optimization processes and continuous technical surveillance. More than a tool 
support, AI acts as a critical accelerator of the operations lifecycle, 
reducing the gap between strategic design and tactical execution of campaigns.

5. Critical Analysis and Proposed Solutions 
5.1. Critical Analysis: Identification of Bottlenecks 
●​ “Siloization” and Briefing Degradation (Process Entropy): Observed 
a critical disconnect between raw content production (Directors of 
Course) and operational implementation (Marketing). The input flow is 
often inefficient, without technical specifications and subject to chains 
of extensive e-mails, which forces the operational team to act in a 
reactivate and carry out SEO corrections afterwards. 
●​ Ambiguity in Operational Planning and Priorities: Lack of 
transparency in the allocation of workloads and visibility of the 
cross-cutting backlog in tools like Asana generates a reactive environment 
focused on "putting out fires", limiting the strategic effectiveness of 
execution. 
●​ "False Economy" and Technical Debt: The use of heterogeneous software and the 
lack of unified integration creates severe technical debt that penalizes 
daily collaboration (e.g. incompatibilities between Affinity and Illustrator). O 
cost in productivity of resolving these frictions is vastly higher than the 
value of unified professional licensing.

Conclusions 
Upon completing the 650 hours of internship at the University's Marketing Unit 
Lusófona, the fundamental conclusion is the validation of the robustness of the curricular plan of the 
CTeSP. Professional practice proved to be a faithful mirror of theory, differentiating itself from 
only on the scale and restrictions typical of a corporate environment. The transition 
from the classroom to the “trench” of a centralized department confirmed that 
the core of the operation is, effectively, the same; the difference lies in the capacity 
of dealing with the variables and limitations that the "real world" imposes. 
My evolution throughout this period transcended the mere execution of tasks. 
The internship acted as an accelerator of technical maturity and pragmatism 
operational. I leave this experience with a level of confidence in my abilities 
significantly superior technology, manifested even in the transition to 
more open and technical operating system environments, such as Linux, and in 
implementing local AI models as an integral part of my workflow 
diary. I leave with a keen awareness that organization and clarity in 
everyday life are the foundations of efficiency. Where before I saw only the need to 
"do", today I see the imperative of "automating and simplifying". The experience 
allowed me to filter out the noise and focus on what really generates value: efficiency in 
mundane tasks to free up time for strategy. 
Finally, this journey reinforced my philosophical conviction about the area: the 
Modern Digital Marketing is a discipline that flourishes through experience 
technique and empirical experimentation, far surpassing the static consultation of 
theoretical manuals. The maxim that marketing is a "show me, 
don't tell me" (show me, don't tell me) was confirmed daily. The effectiveness does not 
resides in the rhetoric about what should be done, but in the technical capacity to do it. 
execute, measure and optimize. I leave this stage not as a spectator of the 
strategy, but as an executor who deeply understands the mechanics 
that makes the gear turn.
