1. Brand Context 
1.1 History of Koenigsegg Automotive AB 
Koenigsegg Automotive AB is a Swedish manufacturer of high-performance hypercars. 
performance, founded in 1994 by Christian von Koenigsegg, then just 22 years old. 
Driven by a passion for automotive engineering, his goal was to create the ultimate sports car 
perfect. The company's headquarters are located in Ängelholm, Sweden, in a former military hangar, 
currently transformed into a technological center of excellence. 
Inspired by models such as the McLaren F1 and the Ferrari F40, Christian developed 
the first prototype, the Koenigsegg CC, in 1996. The first production model, the CC8S, 
was launched in 2002, equipped with a 655 hp V8 engine, and was distinguished by its 
Guinness World Records as the production car with the most powerful engine at the time. 
Since then, the brand has consolidated itself in the hypercar segment, through 
flagship models and pioneering technologies: 
●​ CCX (2006): Developed to comply with United States standards, it allowed 
access to new markets. 
●​ Agera (2010): Reached 1,341 hp and, in 2017, set the world record for 
speed at 447 km/h. 
●​ Regera (2015): Introduced the Koenigsegg Direct Drive (KDD) hybrid system, 
exceeding 1,500 hp. 
●​ Jesko (2019): Focused on track performance, can exceed 1,600 hp. 
●​ Gemera (2020): The brand's first four-seater model, combining comfort, 
innovation and hybrid engine. 
Koenigsegg distinguishes itself through precision engineering, artisanal production and 
constant innovation. Technologies such as dihedral synchro-helix doors, fiberglass chassis 
internally produced carbon and more efficient engines reinforce its status as 
global reference in the sector. Under the visionary leadership of Christian von Koenigsegg, the 
brand remains faithful to its mission: to create the most advanced, exclusive and 
innovators in the world.

1.2 Essence (Brand Key Framework) 
The Brand Key Framework is a structure that helps brands 
be able to define their strategy, tone of voice, among other aspects (Appendix, image 1). 
This structure is made up of 9 components which are: 
The essential strength, which consists of the identity and essence of the brand.  
The competitive environment, which is the analysis of competition in the market where the brand 
is found. 
The ideal target audience, which are the people we want to reach. 
The benefits for the consumer, which is where the brand shows the customer why 
choose this brand over the competition. 
The values, beliefs and personality, which consists of the principles and beliefs that 
will guide the decisions made by the brand. 
And finally we have consumer perception, which is the current image of the brand and how 
the public sees the brand.

Regarding its digital presence, Pagani is active on the six main platforms. 
However, upon initial analysis, it became apparent that only Instagram warranted

The analysis began by identifying the main themes and editorial line

in a more poetic and reserved communication.

1.​ The sophisticated tone reinforces the artistic and premium perception of the brand, but reduces the

2.​ Pagani follows an omnichannel strategy, with uniform content across all channels 
platforms — LinkedIn, Instagram or TikTok — maintaining the same style and 
message, regardless of the target audience or the nature of the network. 
Other relevant observations include the consistency in frequency of 
publication. Although the total number of publications is relatively small, the brand 
publishes weekly, maintaining a regular rhythm. Like Koenigsegg, Pagani does not 
uses paid advertising (paid media), as it does not use social networks as a channel 
direct sales. 
Finally, it was observed that the textual content of publications (copy) is based 
often in testimonials, descriptions of the technical and aesthetic attributes of 
products, as well as in poetic and philosophical phrases with which the CEO himself 
identifies.

2.3 Conclusion to the Initial Analysis 
Therefore, after all the analysis of the brand — in terms of history and essence — combined 
evaluating the marketing mix, social networks and SWOT analysis, not only for Koenigsegg 
as well as the competition, it was possible to derive a series of insights. Some say 
respect for DO’s and DON’Ts in content creation, based on competitive practices 
that demonstrated different levels of effectiveness; others revealed weaknesses and 
opportunities that the brand can and should explore in future campaigns. And finally, it emerged 
a central insight. 
This last insight was decisive for the construction of the entire campaign that was 
follows, offering us a new strategic vision: 
"In the hypercar market, those who see engines don't see hearts. Communication 
It's not human." 
In other words, we found that most competing brands favor 
exaltation of performance and product to the detriment of more emotional communication, 
close and human. This distancing creates an opportunity that Koenigsegg can — and 
must — explore, as it has a differentiating asset: a passionate CEO, with a 
humble and inspiring journey, and a small but united team, made up of professionals 
who love what they do. Each vehicle is custom, unique and hand-built, which 
gives authenticity and emotional value to the brand. 
Given this scenario, and with the conviction that Koenigsegg has a base 
solid and human to distinguish itself, the objective is to reposition the brand's branding. 
Without compromising its essence, we intend to reformulate the message with the following 
slogan: 
Designed and Engineered by Dreamers 
The entire process — from design to production — is led by a team of 
passionate dreamers. A small group, but driven by dedication and pleasure 
genuine for the work he does. This is the message that the brand must convey: it creates 
unique products, with exceptional performance and attention to detail, but none of them would be 
possible without the people behind it. The objective is to value the role of the team and the CEO,

2.4 Target Audience Analysis 
Having already defined the message, we proceeded to analyze who best identifies with 
with her. To this end, three complementary approaches were used: 
1.​ General analysis of social networks, through consultation of studies on the profile of 
users of the platforms in question, with a special focus on age groups and other 
relevant demographic data that would help us guide the investigation; 
2.​ Consultation with Artificial Intelligence (ChatGPT), with the aim of crossing data 
general information with more specific information about the audience in our sector — that of 
hypercars; 
3.​ Social Listening, focused on the social networks under analysis, with the aim of carrying out a 
critical comparison between directly observed data and that provided by the 
ChatGPT. 
The combination of these three strategies was due to the natural limitations of information 
available about our niche. Although there are credible studies from 
recognized sources, most do not directly address the hypercar market, which 
conditions the depth of the analysis. 
As for using ChatGPT, we recognize its advantages — such as 
speed and the ability to synthesize information — but also its limits: it works with 
estimates based on general patterns, common sense, and trained knowledge, which can 
generate plausible answers, but not always exact or rigorous. 
Finally, Social Listening, although considered the most realistic and direct source, 
presents its own limitations: not all platforms provide public profiles 
complete, which makes critical analysis of the comments difficult. Furthermore, the high volume of 
interactions would require exhaustive manual analysis to obtain statistically significant conclusions. 
robust.

to the competition, identified the target audience and developed personas representative of the 
same, and having also determined the most effective platforms for 
communicate with this audience, now is the time to present the campaign where 
all these discoveries and strategic decisions will be put into practice. 
The defined objective is to launch a new vehicle, which is assumed 
as a relevant milestone for Koenigsegg: this will be the tenth model launched to the 
public and coincides with the celebration of the brand's 25 years of existence, a moment 
symbolic that reinforces its legacy and positioning in the hypercar market. 
Before creating the campaign content, it was necessary to structure four 
fundamental elements that ensure the coherence and effectiveness of communication: 
●​ Golden Circle – where it is defined how the brand message will be communicated, 
starting from “why” to “what”; 
●​ Tone of Voice – which guides the style of texts to be used, aligning language and 
emotion with the new human positioning of the brand;​
Mood Board – which establishes a coherent and controlled aesthetic line for all 
the campaign’s visual pieces; 
●​ SMART objectives – which allow you to measure the success of the campaign through goals 
specific and concrete KPIs. 
In the following subtopics, we detail each of these strategic pillars. 
2.5.1 Golden Circle 
The Golden Circle is a framework used to define communication in a 
brand. He argues that this must be built from the inside out, that is, it must start 
for communicating your values and purpose before presenting your products or 
services. This approach is particularly effective because it strengthens the emotional relationship 
with the public, creating a more human, authentic and differentiating brand image. Node

In the case of Koenigsegg, this philosophy communicates not only what the brand sells, but why 
exists, which is especially relevant for such a unique and artisanal brand. 
Based on this logic, we define Koenigsegg's Golden Circle as follows 
shape: 
●​ Why? (Why)​
 Because the CEO, Christian von Koenigsegg, wanted to bring his dream of creating the 
maximum exponent of automotive performance, without concessions, and offer 
customers the possibility of being part of this dream. 
●​ How? (How)​
 Through a tireless pursuit of innovation and perfection, with a particular focus 
in-house development, which allows absolute control over each 
detail. This process is combined with a concern for sustainability, 
giving his creations the status of technological works of art, produced

2.5.4 SMART objectives 
Having as main goal the conversion of followers into participants in the event 
online, three strategic objectives were defined that will allow us to evaluate 
campaign performance over the two weeks is measurable. These objectives 
are organized according to the stages of the marketing funnel, ensuring a 
structured and conversion-oriented. 
1st Objective – Increase the number of followers on social media by 10% 
main ones (LinkedIn, Instagram and YouTube)​
This objective is part of the TOFU stage, where the focus is on capturing attention and 
increased brand awareness. The aim is to reach a wider audience, expanding 
the community that follows Koenigsegg and generating recognition for the launch 
of the new vehicle. 
2nd Objective – Obtain a CTR rate of 15% for the landing page, 
arising from interactions with each campaign publication.​
This objective operates in the MOFU phase, focusing on generating interest and engagement 
with the content disclosed. By encouraging clicks on publications, the aim is to drive 
the target audience to the event landing page, allowing an effective transition between interest and 
the intention. 
3rd Objective – Convert 30% of landing page visitors into registrations on the website 
event and, consequently, in the brand's newsletter.​

2.6.2.2 Editorial Content 
24 years ago, on September 30th, I was backstage at the Paris Auto Show, waiting 
for the CC8S to be wheeled out to the crowd, eager to see everyone’s reaction to this creation 
that me and the team worked so hard to unveil. 
At that moment, I didn't think about the possibilities and what could be; I simply 
wanted people to see it, wanted people to get excited for this machine and this dream that 
began as a couple of rough sketches done in my parent’s bedroom. 
No thoughts of the CCR, the Agera, or the Jesko, no thoughts of Freevalve or DDT, 
nor any other models and technologies our company has produced over the years. A few 
people have come and gone, some teams changed, some expanded, and some became their 
own thing, like Freevalve. 
Eight models were released since that pleasant September day: 
1. 2002 – Koenigsegg CC8S 
2. 2004 – Koenigsegg CCR 
3. 2006 – Koenigsegg CCX 
4. 2011 – Koenigsegg Agera 
5. 2015 – Koenigsegg Agera RS 
6. 2016 – Koenigsegg Regera 
7. 2021 – Koenigsegg Jesko 
8. 2022 – Koenigsegg Gemera 
9. 2023 – Koenigsegg CC850 
And what a ride it has been, with every following release. Koenigsegg has continued 
to push the envelope when it comes to performance and driving experience, always looking 
to improve on what’s been done. 
We have seen many competitors come and go, others keep pushing and being pushed 
by, never letting them rest on their laurels and hoping they do the same for us. 
And that brings us to today. I’m not one much for sentimentalism, but even I know 
the importance of a 25-year anniversary, a quarter century of car manufacturing and 
automobile development that started as an idea in the mind of a young Swedish boy. 
We don’t plan to let a date of such importance go by without recognizing it, and 
certainly not without something worthy of such a date. And so, with that idea in mind, 
Koenigsegg will be present at the 2025 Paris Auto Show, on September 30th, at the exact 
same spot and show where it all started for young Christian von Koenigsegg. 
Thank you for taking this journey with us and “KVARTSSEKELsjubileum.” 
Christian Von Koenigsegg
