01 INTRODUCTION 
 
This work aims to carry out an analysis that seeks to compare the 
online presence (in the field of online advertising with an exclusive focus on Google 
Search Ads) from two direct competitors of the chosen brand (OMEGA) for the project 
final, which in this case are: Rolex and Grand Seiko. 
These brands have markedly different positions with regards to the Ethos of 
watchmaking that they design, produce and sell, Ethos esse, which is also 
manifests itself in the way they build their Ads, from Headlines to Descriptions. 
For analysis purposes, a Paid Search Ad was chosen from each of these 
companies that were online on 3/11/2025. 
 
02 CONTEXTUALIZATION 
OF THE SECTOR  
 
The luxury watchmaking sector operates at a unique intersection of engineering, art and 
psychology. Essentially, these brands do not sell the function of measuring time, 
but rather aspiration, a tangible symbol of success, heritage and status. This reality 
dictates that marketing is mainly based on building desire and 
trust, which explains the inversion of the traditional funnel: TOFU (Awareness) is 
typically built through expensive and emotional offline channels (sponsorships, 
elite events), while MOFU and BOFU (Consideration and Action) are managed with 
surgical precision in digital. The accelerated transition to e-commerce, amplified 
due to recent events, has forced brands like Rolex to defend their image with 
even more rigor, and growing brands like Grand Seiko using digital 
scientific and aggressive way to convert high purchase intention into sales 
immediate.

05 FUNNEL PHASE 
 
In the world of luxury watchmaking (or any good of this kind) people who 
do not know or buy our product are as important as those that 
do, it is this reality that makes the appeal to aspirations so strong and effective, for this reason 
reason, most of the budget of these brands is spent creating this aspiration (Phase 
of Awareness) and outside the “GSA”, leaving Google Search for the 
Consideration and Action. 
 
While in the intricacies of traditional marketing a wider “net” is cast 
due to the fact that these means deal with the inherent difficulties of segmentation, 
in digital, this filtering can be performed without major difficulties. 
In the case of these two brands and, by extension, their competitors, the change of 
paradigm brought by COVID, dictated that the transition to a more 
dependent on digital happened sooner than perhaps they would have wanted. 
 
This funnel distinction is crucial: the content of Print Ads (TOFU), which builds the aura 
of exclusivity, is the aspirational promise that the GSA has to fulfill. Analyzing the 
press campaigns, it is clear that Rolex (through its 
explorer/conquest) carries its promise of timeless authority 
directly to digital, refusing the explicit transaction as in its 
press campaigns. Grand Seiko (with its focus on subtle art and nature) 
uses its artisanal reputation (the Ethos built offline) as the final argument of 
sale in GSA.  
What the customer sees in the magazine (the Aspiration) has to be validated by what the customer sees 
on Google (Execution). Online advertising thus becomes the functional extension of 
narrative established in traditional channels. 
 
The addition of the digital aspect to operations required brands to make the decision 
where this medium fit in, whether in a phase of awareness, consideration or action and 
This analysis will demonstrate that Rolex chose to use the “GSA” as a point of 
primary contact in the Consideration phase, MOFU, focusing on the Image 
Corporate. 
While Grand Seiko, focusing on market growth, integrated the GSA 
as a crucial channel of Action, BOFU, albeit with a secondary objective of 
bring some notoriety to the brand outside of Asia.
